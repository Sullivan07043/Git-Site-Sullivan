About

I'm Shuhao Zhang, a Graduate Student Researcher and M.S. student in Computer Science and Engineering at UC San Diego, advised by Prof. Pengtao Xie. Before that I studied Big Data Management and Application at the University of Science and Technology Beijing.

My research studies how an agent processes its environment, experience, and memory into abstract understanding. This understanding is what gives an agent a real grasp of the world it acts in, and what lets it keep improving the decisions and actions it takes. Carried far enough, the same ability should let a system understand its own architecture and behavior, and improve those as well.

My published work so far sits at the front of that loop. One line estimates how a decision will turn out before making it. The other gets a model to use what it already knows about its own reliability. My current work turns to understanding itself. It recovers the causal structure of a system from its measurements and reads what each hidden variable means.

I'm currently looking for PhD positions.

How Causality Bridges the Semantic Gap

Shuhao Zhang, Xuran Zhou, Han Guo, Pengtao Xie, Yujia Zheng

ICLR 2027 Under Review

Measurements record how a system behaves, yet they seldom say what its variables mean. CausalBridge reads the meaning of each unnamed variable from the causal role that it plays, and it names observed and latent variables in questionnaires and robot systems more accurately than methods that rely on association. It is a step toward agents that form a causal and abstract understanding of the environment they observe.

Send a SCOUT First: Pre-hoc Reasoning for Adaptive Detector Allocation in Prompt-Injection Defense

Shuhao Zhang†, Jiarui Li†, Qi Cao, Ruiyi Zhang, Pengtao Xie

EMNLP 2026 Accepted (Main)

Reframed prompt-injection defense as per-input detector allocation. SCOUT summarizes each detector's behavior as a fingerprint over a fixed anchor set, then trains a small predictor (Qwen3-4B; SFT + GRPO) to estimate, for every new request, which detectors are reliable and how long each will take. A single operator threshold trades safety vs. latency. Cuts attack-success rate by 46% and wall-clock by 40% vs. an always-on GPT-4o judge, at a 5.1-point drop in benign utility, and transfers to BIPIA, IPI, and IHEval.

Models Under SCOPE: Scalable and Controllable Routing via Pre-hoc Reasoning

Qi Cao†, Shuhao Zhang†, Ruizhe Zhou, Ruiyi Zhang, Peijia Qin, Pengtao Xie

ICML 2026 Accepted

SCOPE is a budget-aware LLM routing framework that predicts how accurate and how expensive each model will be before running it, enabling controllable cost–accuracy trade-offs and naturally handling new models. Up to 95.1% cost reduction or 25.7% accuracy gain under different priorities.

LLMs Know When They Know, but Do Not Act on It: A Metacognitive Harness for Test-time Scaling

Qi Cao, Yufan Wang, Peijia Qin, Shuhao Zhang, Pengtao Xie

Preprint 2026

A metacognitive harness that separates monitoring from reasoning. The model emits a pre-solve feeling-of-knowing and a post-solve judgment-of-learning signal, turned into an explicit test-time control interface for retry or aggregation. On a fixed Claude Sonnet-4.6 base, raised pooled accuracy from 48.3 to 56.9 without any parameter updates, exceeding the strongest leaderboard entries on HLE-Verified, LiveCodeBench v6, and R-Bench-V.

IDC-CDR: Cross-domain Recommendation based on Intent Disentanglement and Contrast Learning

Jing Xu, Mingxin Gan, Hang Zhang, Shuhao Zhang

Information Processing and Management 2024

A cross-domain recommendation model that disentangles user intent for stronger transfer, with an emphasis on interpretability of latent intent factors.
